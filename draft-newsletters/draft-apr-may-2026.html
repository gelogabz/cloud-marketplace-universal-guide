Faster Private Offers and Lower Renewal Fees: Deal Mechanics Changed

Three updates that reduce time-to-close and improve net margin on marketplace deals

Private offer mechanics across AWS, Azure, and GCP all changed in the past 12 months — separately, on different timelines, but pointing in the same direction: making enterprise deals faster to close, cheaper to renew, and more channel-friendly. Each update is specific enough to affect how you structure your next deal.

AWS

Express Private Offers + Installment CPPOs: Two Deal Friction Points Removed

AWS launched Express Private Offers in November 2025, cutting private offer creation time from hours to minutes for sellers managing high-velocity pipeline. Flexible payment scheduling expanded to Channel Partner Private Offers in February 2025, letting resellers structure installment-based deals directly in the marketplace without going off-platform. Together, these close the two most common reasons complex enterprise deals left the marketplace: setup friction and the inability to match enterprise payment terms. Apply Express Private Offers to any time-sensitive opportunity; enable installment CPPOs for any channel deal where annual upfront pricing is the blocker.

AWS Marketplace Private Offers
Azure

30-Day Offer Expiry + 1.5% Renewal Fee: Two Changes That Improve Your Margin

Azure extended private offer expiry from 14 to 30 days, giving enterprise buyers more runway for internal approval processes without the offer lapsing mid-cycle. On renewals, Microsoft's store service fee dropped to 1.5% — half the standard 3% rate — for the full renewal term. On a $2M annual renewal, that's $30K in additional margin that doesn't require any pricing change on your end. Update any CRM automation, expiry alerts, or notification rules still calibrated to the old 14-day window — those will misfire under the new default.

Azure Marketplace Transaction Capabilities
GCP

MCPO 100% Commit Drawdown: Channel Deals Now Draw Down Like Direct Purchases

GCP's Marketplace Channel Private Offers have qualified for 100% commit drawdown against customer GCP committed spend since June 2025 — capped at 25% of the total commitment, same rate as direct GCP purchases. GCP's variable revenue share model, effective April 2025, also charges 1.5% on renewals (down from 3% on new transactions), improving ISV net margin on the renewal side of your GCP book. Combined, channel-transacted GCP deals are now as financially attractive to buyers as direct purchases, and cheaper for ISVs to process on renewal volume. Ask your channel team whether buyers have committed GCP spend — if yes, the MCPO drawdown eligibility is a closing argument.

GCP Marketplace Channel Private Offers
What this means for you

ISVs who've been routing complex deals off-marketplace to handle installments, channel margin, or approval timelines should revisit those assumptions — the mechanics have caught up to enterprise deal reality. The practical step is enabling your channel partners on CPPO (AWS), MPO (Azure), and MCPO (GCP) if they aren't already transacting through those mechanisms, and confirming your CRM logic reflects the new Azure expiry window. None of this requires a product change — it's a process and enablement update.

What Is Committed Spend — and Why It's Your Most Underused Closing Tool

EDP, MACC, and GCP committed spend, explained for sales teams

An enterprise buyer tells you they love your product but needs another quarter to get budget approved. What they may not have mentioned: they have $8M in AWS committed spend they're behind on drawing down. If your product is on AWS Marketplace, that budget problem changes entirely — their procurement team has a financial incentive to buy on-marketplace that bypasses the usual approval cycle. This is committed spend, and it's the most underused closing tool available to marketplace ISVs.

AWS

EDP: Enterprise Discount Program

AWS's Enterprise Discount Program (EDP) is a multi-year contract where enterprises commit to a minimum annual AWS spend in exchange for discounted cloud rates. Software purchased through AWS Marketplace counts toward the customer's EDP drawdown target — meaning marketplace transactions help buyers hit their commitment without additional infrastructure spend. For ISVs, EDP-eligible buyers have a financial incentive to buy on-marketplace that exists independent of your deal terms. The qualifying question to work into every enterprise discovery call: "Do you have an AWS EDP, and are you on track to draw it down?" A buyer behind on drawdown has urgency that your price negotiation doesn't create.

AWS Marketplace — What Is It?
Azure

MACC: Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitments

Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitments (MACC) works similarly: enterprise customers commit to minimum Azure spend, and purchases through Azure Marketplace reduce their MACC balance. The key constraint is ISV eligibility — only ISVs with Azure IP Co-sell Incentivized status can qualify for MACC drawdown. If your product isn't MACC-eligible, you can't use the buyer's committed spend as a closing lever, and a competitor who is MACC-eligible has a structural advantage in any account where the buyer has unspent balance. Check Partner Center for your current co-sell status — the gap between Eligible and Incentivized is the most common stall point for Azure ISVs.

Azure Consumption Commitment Enrollment
GCP

GCP Committed Spend: 100% Drawdown Through MCPO Since June 2025

GCP's committed spend program lets customers apply GCP Marketplace purchases against their committed spend obligations, up to a 25% cap. Since June 2025, qualifying MCPO channel purchases count toward drawdown at 100% — the same rate as direct purchases — making channel deals as commitment-friendly as direct ones. GCP's committed spend pool is smaller in absolute terms than AWS EDP or Azure MACC, but it's growing alongside GCP's AI infrastructure adoption, which is driving larger enterprise GCP commits as AI workload spend increases. For every enterprise prospect with GCP committed spend, ask your channel team whether the deal qualifies for MCPO before structuring it as a direct offer.

GCP Marketplace SaaS Listing Guide
What this means for you

The committed spend question is simple enough to work into any discovery call: "Are you working toward a cloud commitment with AWS, Azure, or GCP?" The answer tells you whether you're selling against a budget constraint or alongside a financial incentive that's independent of your deal terms. ISVs who build this into their qualification process consistently find that enterprise buyers with committed spend have shorter sales cycles on-marketplace — because the purchase maps to something the buyer already needs to do. If your sales team isn't asking this question on every enterprise call, that changes this week.

30 days after GCP's Agent Marketplace launch, early signals on what converts and what doesn't

Three Marketplace Fee Changes That Directly Affect Your Net Revenue

GCP's variable rate, Azure's renewal discount, and AWS's partial disbursements — modeled

Marketplace fee structures across AWS, Azure, and GCP all changed in the past 12 months. None were heavily publicized — but each one directly affects ISV net revenue on marketplace deals. If your deal desk is still modeling fees at pre-2025 rates, your margin calculations are wrong.

AWS

Partial Disbursement Support: Cash Flow Improves on Installment Deals

AWS added partial disbursement support in May 2025: sellers now receive funds proportionally as buyers make partial payments on installment deals, rather than waiting for full invoice settlement. For enterprise deals on net-60 or net-90 payment terms with installment structures, this materially reduces the receivables lag — funds arrive in stages rather than in a single payment after the customer's final installment clears. Model your cash flow projections using partial release timelines, not total invoice settlement dates, especially for deals over $500K with multi-year installment structures where the difference from the old model can reach 60–90 days of working capital.

AWS Marketplace Disbursement Docs
Azure

Renewal Private Offer Fee at 1.5%: Half the New Transaction Rate

Microsoft's store service fee for Azure Marketplace renewal private offers dropped to 1.5% in 2025 — half the standard 3% rate on new transactions — for the full renewal term. This applies to renewal private offers specifically; new transaction offers remain at 3%. On a $1M annual renewal, the savings versus treating it as a new transaction is $15K in platform fees with no pricing change to the customer. Structure long-term customer agreements as renewal private offers wherever the offer terms allow; this is the highest-leverage fee optimization available on Azure for ISVs with established customer relationships.

Azure Marketplace Payout Schedule
GCP

Variable Revenue Share: 3% New, 1.5% Renewal, Lower for Channel

Google Cloud's variable revenue share model, effective April 21, 2025, charges 3% on new marketplace transactions and 1.5% on renewals, with further reductions possible for channel shifts and migrations per the Vendor Net Revenue Schedule. The applicable rate is determined per transaction by Google's system — ISVs don't self-select — based on deal type and total contract value. For ISVs with significant renewal and channel volume on GCP, the effective blended rate across their book is materially below 3%. Ask your GCP partner development manager for a Vendor Net Revenue Schedule review if you haven't had that conversation since April 2025.

GCP Vendor Net Revenue Schedule
What this means for you

Across all three marketplaces, renewal economics are now materially better than new transaction economics. ISVs building their marketplace pricing strategy should model the renewal fee rate as the steady-state cost of their business, not the new transaction rate — because the revenue that matters for long-term margin is the renewal book. Deal desks should add marketplace fee as a named line item in their P&L models, using the applicable rate per deal type, not a single blended estimate. At $10M in annual marketplace renewal volume, the difference between a 3% and a 1.5% effective rate is $150K in margin you're either capturing or leaving on the table.

What Co-Sell Ready Actually Means — Most ISVs Aren't There

The difference between having a marketplace listing and having a co-sell motion

Your team got the AWS Marketplace listing live six months ago. You checked the Partner Center profile box for Azure. You submitted the GCP Partner Advantage application. And yet the hyperscaler field team isn't engaging your pipeline. The problem is almost always the same: having a listing and being co-sell ready are two different things, and the gap between them is operational, not technical.

AWS

ACE Pipeline Activity Is the Trigger — Not Listing Status

Co-sell readiness on AWS requires APN membership at Select tier or above, an active ACE pipeline with regularly submitted opportunities, Marketplace Transaction Revenue history demonstrating you close deals on-marketplace — and ideally a Foundational Technical Review for the AWS Qualified Software badge. ISV Accelerate program access, which gates access to AWS field seller introductions and funded POC support, requires meeting these prerequisites. The common failure mode: submitting ACE opportunities at launch and going quiet. AWS co-sell engagement is allocated based on current pipeline activity, not historical submissions. Someone on your team needs to own ACE as a weekly process — reviewing pipeline, submitting new opportunities, and closing out won/lost deals — not a quarterly task.

AWS Partner Central — ACE Program
Azure

The Eligible-to-Incentivized Gap Is Where Most ISVs Stall

Azure co-sell readiness follows a specific sequence: publish a transact offer → complete Partner Center profile → hit business KPIs → achieve IP Co-sell Eligible → sustain transaction volume → achieve IP Co-sell Incentivized. MACC eligibility — enabling your customers to draw down Azure committed spend on your product — requires Incentivized status, not just Eligible. Most ISVs stall between Eligible and Incentivized because they're not actively driving marketplace transaction volume, which is the primary KPI Microsoft uses to advance co-sell status. The fix is not technical: it's dedicating someone to Partner Center reporting, referral submission, and deal closure tracking on a weekly cadence. Check your Partner Center dashboard for the specific metric gap between your current status and Incentivized.

Azure IP Co-sell Requirements
GCP

GCP Rewards Customer Success Reporting, Not Just Bookings

GCP co-sell engagement is unlocked at Partner status in the Sell track of Partner Advantage, with Premier Partner status adding a dedicated PDM and funded campaigns. Unlike AWS and Azure, GCP measures ISV performance through Customer Success Plans (CSPs) tied to adoption outcomes — quantified customer wins, not just transaction volume. ISVs who report documented customer adoption metrics in their GCP engagement get co-sell prioritization that transact-volume-only reporters don't. The practical gap: GCP Partner Advantage requires an active reporting cadence and documented customer wins; ISVs who don't invest in that process don't get the field engagement that depends on it. If you don't have a quarterly Partner Advantage review scheduled with your GCP PDM, that's the first gap to close.

Google Cloud Partner Advantage
What this means for you

Co-sell readiness is an operational discipline, not a configuration state. The ISVs generating real hyperscaler co-sell pipeline have a person who owns ACE, Partner Center, and GCP Partner Advantage as a primary responsibility — not a secondary one shared with three other jobs. If your organization treats co-sell program management as a part-time assignment, you're leaving significant pipeline on the table every quarter. The fix isn't technical: it's headcount allocation and process ownership. A single dedicated co-sell program manager, measured on incremental pipeline driven through hyperscaler co-sell, is one of the highest-ROI hires a mid-stage marketplace ISV can make in 2026.
