01 News Featured AWS

AWS Marketplace now transacts AI agents

The agent economy gets a buy button — and a new set of questions for ISVs about pricing and co-sell

AWS made its AI Agents store transactional this week. ISVs can now list Bedrock-powered agent products with pay-per-use billing directly through AWS Marketplace — complete with private offer support, MACC drawdown eligibility, and ACE co-sell attribution. Until now, agent listings were discoverable but not directly purchasable through the marketplace transaction layer. That distinction just disappeared.

AWS Transactional API is live, private offers work, MACC counts

The new AWS Marketplace AI Agents store uses the same Marketplace Catalog API that ISVs already use for SaaS listings, with two additions: a usage-event endpoint for per-invocation billing and a product type declaration of AI_AGENT. Private offer mechanics carry over unchanged — partners can create custom pricing for enterprise buyers, and those transactions draw down MACC balances as normal. ACE co-sell attribution is supported from day one [VERIFY: confirm ACE sync is live, not just "planned"].

For ISVs without an AI agent product yet: watch your competitors' listing strategies. 02 News ⏰ Time-sensitive Azure

Microsoft extends MACC double-drawdown — 55 days left

The incentive that made Azure Marketplace deals close faster is still on through June 30. Here's why it matters for end-of-quarter.

Microsoft confirmed the MACC double-drawdown incentive runs through June 30, 2026 — meaning marketplace transactions generated through a co-sell motion count as two times their transaction value against a customer's Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment. The program was originally introduced in Q3 FY2025 and has been extended twice. No commitment to extend beyond June 30 has been made.

Azure How double-drawdown works and what qualifies

A customer with a $10M MACC balance who buys a $500K ISV product through Azure Marketplace gets $1M credited against their MACC — not $500K. The doubling applies to transactions where an active Azure co-sell opportunity in Partner Center was involved and the deal was transacted through Marketplace (not direct billing). ISVs need to ensure their ACE opportunity is logged and co-sell eligible before the transaction closes. Deals submitted after June 30 don't qualify, even if the negotiation started earlier [VERIFY: whether in-flight deals before June 30 qualify if they close July 1].

AWS No equivalent AWS program active — EDP drawdown mechanics are different

AWS's EDP (Enterprise Discount Program) works differently. Marketplace transactions draw down the committed spend balance at 1:1 — there's no current multiplier incentive equivalent to MACC double-drawdown. AWS occasionally runs co-sell spiff programs through APN, but none with a published drawdown multiplier as of this week. Worth flagging to customers evaluating AWS vs. Azure timing for large enterprise deals.

What this means for your pipeline

Any Azure deal currently in late-stage negotiation with a MACC-eligible customer should be explicitly evaluated for double-drawdown eligibility before June 30. If the transaction can route through Marketplace with an active co-sell opportunity, the effective value to the buyer doubles from a committed spend perspective — which is a real closing argument, especially for customers managing Q2 fiscal year-end. Your Microsoft PDM should be able to confirm co-sell eligibility in Partner Center within 24 hours.

03 Educational AWS

AWS ISV Accelerate explained — and what the new AI-Native track means for you

Co-sell benefits, eligibility tiers, and why the AI-Native track changes the ARR math for AI-first companies

ISV Accelerate is AWS's primary co-sell program for independent software vendors. Being enrolled unlocks AWS field seller collaboration, deal registration through ACE (AWS Customer Engagements), and in some tiers, co-sell funding. The program matters because co-sell deals close at a higher rate than direct ISV-led deals — AWS field sellers actively refer enrolled ISVs when the product fits a customer's use case. The new AI-Native track, announced April 28, changes the entry criteria for AI-focused companies.

Program tiers and what they unlock

Enrolled Any marketplace listing Same ACE access, basic co-sell badge

How enrollment works

ISV Accelerate enrollment starts in AWS Partner Central. You need an active APN membership, at least one published AWS Marketplace listing, and a completed co-sell profile. AWS typically reviews applications within 2–3 weeks. The AI-Native track adds one additional qualification: products must derive more than 60% of their feature set from generative AI capabilities on Bedrock or SageMaker [VERIFY: exact percentage threshold]. If you're already enrolled at Select or Top Tier under the standard track, you can request a re-assessment under the AI-Native criteria.

Getting the most out of ACE

ACE (AWS Customer Engagements) is where co-sell actually happens. Once enrolled, you can submit deal registrations to AWS — attaching a customer account, deal size, and timeline — and AWS field sellers can either endorse the deal (making you "co-sell eligible" for that opportunity) or refer new opportunities to you. The quality of your ACE submissions matters: AWS sellers are selective about which ISVs they engage with, and a well-structured deal registration with a clear customer use case performs significantly better than a generic submission.

What this means for your pipeline

If your product is AI-native (built primarily on Bedrock or SageMaker), the new track means you may qualify for Select or Top Tier benefits at half the previously required ARR threshold. That's material — Top Tier access to co-sell funding and joint GTM resources was previously gated behind $1M in marketplace ARR, which was a significant barrier for early-stage AI companies. The announcement this week is worth a re-evaluation of your co-sell eligibility if you've been below the standard thresholds.
