Suger Intelligence · Writing Guideline v1

The one rule that
overrides everything else

This guide is used by AI agents and the content team when generating newsletter content. Every section here is a directive — not a suggestion.

Core principle
Never describe what something is without telling the reader what to do with that information. Every article, update, or section must answer the reader's implicit question: "Why does this matter to me, and what should I do next?"

Why this exists

Suger's readers are busy. They have global purview across multiple clouds, multiple teams, and multiple priorities. They don't have time to read an update and figure out on their own why it matters to them. That work is ours to do — not theirs.

The goal of every piece of content is to make the reader feel like someone who actually understands their job filtered this information specifically for them.

What this is not

✕
A recap of hyperscaler press releases
✕
A collection of links with brief descriptions
✕
Content for everyone, personalized for no one
✕
A blog that publishes whenever something interesting happens

What this is

✓
A weekly intelligence briefing that tells each reader exactly what changed, what it means for their role, and what to do next
✓
A living curriculum that helps readers advance along the marketplace maturity curve
✓
A scaled CS tool — the thing that answers questions before customers have to ask them
Voice & style

Tone & voice

The four principles

✓
Professional but plain. Write like a smart colleague explaining something over Slack, not a press release.
✓
Direct. Lead with the most important thing. Don't bury the point in context.
✓
Grounded. No hype. No filler words. Use concrete numbers and specifics instead of adjectives.
✓
Empathetic. Assume the reader is busy, has global coverage, and is not an expert in everything.

Words to remove on sight

excitingrobustgame-changingtransformativeseamlesslycutting-edgepowerfulinnovativeleverageutilizedeep divein today's landscape

Patterns to avoid

✕
Opening with "This article will cover..." or "In this piece, we explore..."
✕
Describing a feature without explaining its impact on the reader
✕
Passive voice — say who did what
✕
Jargon on first use without a plain-language explanation
✕
Generic intros that could apply to any article
Content type

What Changed

The recency section. Confirmed updates from AWS, Azure, and GCP that affect how users operate on marketplace.

⚡ Recency rule
Only include confirmed changes with a source and date. Never speculation. If it can't be cited, it doesn't go here.

Rules

✓
Lead with the cloud provider and what changed in the first sentence
✓
Second sentence: what this means operationally for the reader
✓
Maximum 3 sentences per item
✓
Always include source and date

Template

// Sentence 1: who did what [Cloud Provider] [changed / launched / updated] [specific thing]. // Sentence 2: operational impact This means [what the reader can now do / no longer has to do / needs to check]. // Sentence 3 (optional): next action [What to do next, or what to watch for.]

Good vs bad

✓ Good
AWS Marketplace launched Resale Authorization for EMEA. Channel partners can now resell ISV listings in EU and UK without a separate listing — cutting time to activate a new regional partner from weeks to days.
✕ Bad
AWS has announced exciting new features for their marketplace platform that will enhance the partner ecosystem experience across regions.
Content type

Deep Dive

The 101–201 curriculum section. Foundational and intermediate concepts that help readers operate more effectively on cloud marketplace.

Rules

✓
Headline names the concept AND the benefit — not just the topic
✓
Open with a scenario the reader will recognize
✓
Explain the concept in plain language, then connect to what the reader should do
✓
Close with a clear "what you should do" or "what to watch for"
✓
Target length: 400–600 words

Headline formula

✓ Good
"What is a co-sell motion — and why does it matter to your quota?"
✕ Bad
"Introduction to Co-Selling with Hyperscalers"

Flow template

// 1. Open with a scenario they'll recognize [Situation the reader has been in or will be in] // 2. Name and explain the concept plainly [What this is, in plain language. No jargon without explanation.] // 3. Show how it works in practice [Concrete example — ideally with a number or timeline] // 4. Tell them what to do with this [Clear next action or thing to watch for]
Content type

Market Pulse

One meaningful data point from the broader cloud market, connected back to the reader's work. A fun fact with a point.

Rules

✓
One stat or finding as the headline
✓
2–3 sentences of context: what's driving it, where it comes from
✓
One sentence connecting it directly to the reader's work

Template

// Headline: the stat itself [Specific number or finding] // Body: context [What's driving this. Where the data comes from.] // Closer: so what? For [persona], this means [direct connection to their work].
Content type

Latest News

Notable news from hyperscalers, the partner ecosystem, or the cloud market that may affect how readers think about their marketplace strategy.

Rules

✓
News-style lead: who, what, when
✓
Second paragraph: what this could mean for the reader — interpretive, not just a summary
✓
Flag whether this is time-sensitive or something to monitor
✕
Don't just restate the press release — add a "so what" for the reader
Persona writing angles

Write for your reader,
not a generic audience

The same update can — and should — be written differently for each persona. Select a persona below to see their priorities, what to avoid, and a writing example.

🤝
Partnerships & Alliances
Co-sell velocity · Partner attach rate · Indirect revenue
What they care about

Making co-sell motions work without manual chaos. They care about partner attach rate, indirect revenue, and opportunity sharing. If something makes activating a new partner faster, they want to know.

Frame content around
co-sell motionpartner attachopportunity sharingactivation timelinespipeline impact
Avoid
Finance-heavy languageCRM admin detailquota-focused framing
Example — AWS EMEA Resale Authorization launch
Topic: AWS now allows channel partners to resell in EMEA without a separate listing
Your channel partners in EU and UK can now resell your listing without you setting up a separate listing per country. That removes one of the most common blockers when activating new regional partners — fewer setup calls, faster time to first deal.
💼
Sales (Account Executives)
Close speed · Committed cloud spend · Zero added friction
What they care about

Closing deals without learning new tools. They want to use buyer's committed cloud spend to unlock budget. If something makes a deal move faster or unblocks an approval, they need it in one sentence.

Frame content around
deal velocitycommitted spendunblocking approvalsCRM-native actions
Avoid
Policy-heavy languagePartnership strategy framingAnything that sounds like overhead
Example — Azure private offer expiry extended to 30 days
Topic: Azure extended private offer expiry window from 14 to 30 days
If you've had buyers let offers expire because 14 days wasn't enough time for internal approvals, Azure just fixed that. The expiry window is now 30 days. Set your offer timelines accordingly and stop losing deals to clock-outs.
📊
Finance & Deal Desk
Revenue recognition · Quote-to-cash · Reconciliation
What they care about

Knowing exactly when and how money moves. They need clean quote-to-cash, accurate invoicing, and audit-ready reconciliation. Vague language loses them immediately — always use exact numbers and timelines.

Frame content around
exact fees & ratesdisbursement timelinesRevRec implicationsERP/CRM workflow
Avoid
Co-sell strategy languageVague "streamlining" claimsAnything without a number
Example — GCP listing fee confirmed unchanged
Topic: GCP transaction fee stays at 3% for Q2 2026
GCP's 3% transaction fee holds for Q2 2026. If you're building revenue recognition models or deal desk pricing for GCP deals this quarter, no rate adjustments needed. Disbursement timelines remain on the standard 30-day cycle.
⚙️
Sales Operations
Process consistency · CRM hygiene · Approval workflows
What they care about

Making sure systems align and processes don't sprawl. Any marketplace change that touches approvals, routing, or CRM configuration is their concern. Frame everything as a process or system implication.

Frame content around
process implicationCRM configurationapproval flow impactdownstream data
Avoid
Sales quota languageStrategic framingAnything without a process implication
Example — Azure private offer expiry extended to 30 days
Topic: Azure extended private offer expiry window from 14 to 30 days
Azure's new 30-day offer expiry default gives your approval workflows more runway. Check any automated expiry alerts, CRM field logic, or notification rules tied to the old 14-day window — those may need to be updated to avoid false triggers.
🔧
Biz Tech & CRM Admins
Integration stability · System architecture · No technical debt
What they care about

Not inheriting someone else's mess. They care about whether a marketplace change requires a configuration update on their end, breaks an integration, or introduces new fields and flows that clutter the existing setup.

Frame content around
system-level changeintegration implicationsconfig action requiredfield logic review
Avoid

Azure's offer expiry default changed from 14 to 30 days. If you have automation or field logic in your CRM tied to offer expiry dates — triggers, alerts, stage transitions — verify those rules still fire correctly under the new window.

Quality checklist

Run every piece of content through this before it's finalized. The agent should use this as a self-review step after generating a draft.

Agent instruction
After generating any draft, re-read the content and verify each item below. If any item fails, revise before returning the output.
Does the headline name both the topic and the benefit?
Does the first sentence lead with the most important thing?
Is there a clear "so what" — not just a description of what happened?
Is the content framed for the tagged persona, not a generic reader?
Are all claims in "What Changed" sourced and confirmed (no speculation)?
Is the length appropriate for the content type?
Has all jargon been explained on first use?
Have filler words (exciting, robust, game-changing) been removed?
